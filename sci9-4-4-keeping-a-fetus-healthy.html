    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 4 Maintaining life, section 4.4 Keeping a fetus healthy, pages 151–159; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 77–82. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. Activity 4.4.1 (a group display about diet during pregnancy), the section project on how scientists linked smoking to health, and the unit check-your-progress pages are classroom discussion, research and review tasks and are not part of this interactive lesson — except that the nutrient table from check-your-progress question 4 (recommended daily protein 45→60 g, calcium 0.8→1.2 g, iron 15→30 mg) is used in step 3.

    Objectives (from the book): think about how the development of a fetus is affected by the health of the mother; find out how diet, smoking and drugs can affect fetal health. Key words: fetal, fetus — plus placenta, diffuse, nutrient, haemoglobin, carbon monoxide, nicotine, drug and correlation.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D clinic bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'In the clinic' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the Getting started recall of the Stage 8 nutrients opens the diet step; exchange across the placenta comes first (oxygen and glucose in, carbon dioxide and urea back — the two bloods never mix), then diet with the book's own recommended amounts, then smoking (carbon monoxide and nicotine cross too, and carbon monoxide cuts the oxygen haemoglobin can carry), then drugs and the book's recommendations. The exercises use the Workbook's own data: 4.4A mammal pregnancy lengths, 4.4B the Swedish caffeine study (1037 women; group means 3660 g, 3661 g, 3597 g, 3694 g), and 4.4C the Japanese smoking study (53 386 births; low birthweight 14.0% vs 9.6% in 2004–2005 and 14.5% vs 9.9% in 2006–2007).
