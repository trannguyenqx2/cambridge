    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 5 Reactivity, section 5.5 Rearranging atoms, pages 184–196; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 101–108. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The section project 'Where is the evidence?' (advertising claims, pages 193–194) and the unit check-your-progress pages are classroom research and review tasks and are not part of this interactive lesson; the Getting started partner paper-swap is adapted here as a formula/name card sort.

    Objectives (from the book): look at the rearrangement of atoms in chemical reactions; learn what happens to the mass of reactants and products in a reaction; learn what happens to the energy involved in chemical reactions; carry out practical work safely. Key words: crucible, endothermic reactions, exothermic reaction, the law of conservation of energy, the law of conservation of mass.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D chemistry bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera. The steps follow the book's order and its own values: iron + sulfur rearranges into iron sulfide; the atom count Mg + 2HCl balances (Mg 1 = 1, H 2 = 2, Cl 2 = 2); a stoppered flask keeps the balance reading unchanged (37 g + 150 g → 187 g of products); Arun's open flask falls from 250 g to 207 g as carbon dioxide escapes; burning magnesium in a crucible gains mass as oxygen joins (Lavoisier, 1772); potassium + water is exothermic and the sherbet reaction endothermic. The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks and data: 5.5A atom counts and the 10 g + 6 g → 16 g and 15 g + 9 g → 24 g iron–sulfur masses, 5.5B element and mass statements (including the 45 g and 250 g → 247 g cases), and 5.5C the limited-oxygen burning data (compound mass levelling at 3.4 g).
