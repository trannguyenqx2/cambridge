    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 3 Rocks, the rock cycle and soil, section 3.1, pages 63–69; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 37–39. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The book's Getting started questions about the layers of the Earth need the book's printed diagram, and collecting real rock samples outdoors is a classroom activity — neither is part of this interactive lesson.

    Objectives: find out that rocks can be classified into different types and describe the features of each; use diagrams and photographs to describe igneous rocks; describe the formation of igneous rocks in terms of magma and solidification; make predictions based on how igneous rocks form, and describe their accuracy; describe risks when planning practical work and work safely; sort and group rocks by observing differences; complete a key to igneous rocks based on easily observed differences. Key words: crystal, crystalline, extrusive igneous rock, intrusive igneous rock, mineral, naked eye, geologist, igneous rock, sedimentary rock, solidification.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping things in the 3D rock scene itself, or sorting cards. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'In the rock scene' panel only move the camera to look at a thing; finding means tapping the thing in the scene. The lesson follows the book's order: sorting collected rocks by looking at them, the magma-and-lava diagram, intrusive and extrusive rocks, the minerals inside granite, the salt-crystal investigation (jar 2 in a warm place, jar 1 in the fridge — exactly as the book sets it up), and finally the key to igneous rocks.
