    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 2 Properties of materials, section 2.4 Simple and giant structures, pages 64–76; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 37–41. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The unit project (Building the Periodic Table) and the unit check-your-progress pages are classroom research and review tasks covering the whole unit, and are not part of this interactive lesson.

    Objectives (from the book): learn how giant structures are formed; compare the properties of ionic and covalent substances; explain how the structures of these substances relate to their properties. Key words: lattice, intermolecular forces, graphite, layers — plus electrostatic forces, ionic bond, covalent bond and macromolecule.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; testing a structure means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the sodium chloride lattice from the Getting started discussion, simple molecules (carbon dioxide and oxygen), the giant carbon structures diamond and graphite (Activity 2.4.1's model-building, done here with 3D models), the book's melting-point data table (sodium chloride 801 °C, magnesium chloride 714 °C, calcium oxide 2613 °C, methane −182 °C, ammonia −77 °C, chlorine −101 °C), and the Think like a scientist conductivity investigation — crystal first (no conduction), then solution (the bulb lights).
