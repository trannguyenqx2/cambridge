    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 5 Reactivity, section 5.1 Reactivity and displacement reactions, pages 160–165; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 83–88. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The partner poster and mnemonic work of Activity 5.1.1 and the full six-metal bar-chart table of Workbook 5.1C are classroom tasks; the ordering-cards part of Activity 5.1.1 is step 2 here, and the Workbook challenge word equations are Exercise 3.

    Objectives (from the book's summary): use the reactivity series of metals to predict which metals will displace others from a solution of their salts; carry out some displacement reactions safely. Key words: reactivity series, displacement reaction, salt, sulfate, word equation — plus reactive and displace.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the metal samples and solution tubes on the 3D chemistry bench itself, or sorting cards. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; testing a pair means tapping the metal and the tube on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the reactivity series first (potassium most reactive, gold least), then the book's own displacement example — an iron nail in copper sulfate, which coats the nail with copper (copper sulfate + iron → iron sulfate + copper) — then the reverse test that fails, then the book's 'Displacing metals' investigation pairs (zinc in copper sulfate, magnesium in zinc sulfate, copper in magnesium sulfate), and finally prediction cards. The exercises keep the Workbook's Focus → Practice → Challenge structure: 5.1A yes/no predictions, 5.1B reading the displacement results table, and 5.1C completing word equations.
