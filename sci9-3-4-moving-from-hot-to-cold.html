    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 3 Forces and energy, section 3.4 Moving from hot to cold, pages 105–110; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 55–57. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives (from the book): discover that thermal energy always transfers from hotter places to colder places; understand what is meant by heat dissipation. Key words: hotter, colder — plus thermal energy, dissipation and temperature difference.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the things on the 3D bench itself, or answering the big question the book poses. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; the steps follow the book's order: feeling heat (a hot drink warms your colder hands), the uses on page 106 (a refrigerator cools food; water carries thermal energy away from a car engine), feeling cold (holding ice — cold is not an energy and never flows), the open window and dissipation (with the book's ice cream at −18 °C in a room at 22 °C), the hot-coffee thought experiment of Activity 3.4.1, and the lamp-and-beaker investigation, where the water temperature rises, then stops rising when heating and dissipation balance. Watch out for the two tricky ideas: 'cold' never moves — thermal energy moves away — and dissipated energy is spread out, never destroyed.
