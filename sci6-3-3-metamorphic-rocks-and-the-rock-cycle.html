    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 3 Rocks, the rock cycle and soil, section 3.3, pages 78–86; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 43–45. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: describe the features of metamorphic rocks and how they form; use diagrams and photographs to describe metamorphic rocks; sort and group rocks by observing differences; complete a key to metamorphic rocks based on easily observed differences; describe the rock cycle, and the formation of metamorphic, igneous and sedimentary rocks, in terms of magma, solidification, erosion, sedimentation, burial, metamorphism and melting; describe how a model can help us understand the rock cycle; use reference material to identify rocks used in your community. Key words: burial, gneiss, mass, metamorphic rock, quartzite, marble, slate, pressure, magma, rock cycle, melting, sedimentation.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the heat, pressure and changing zone deep underground, sorting old rocks into the metamorphic rocks they become, using the book's two-question key on a mystery rock, following the rock cycle in order, spotting three metamorphic rocks people use, then sorting changes by the kind of rock they make. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: metamorphic rocks are old rocks changed by heat, by pressure, or by both together, and the rock cycle is a model whose arrows show the order of events, not scale or time.
