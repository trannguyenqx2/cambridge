    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 5 Forces and electricity, section 5.3, pages 124–129; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 68–73. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: find out that the mass and shape of an object can affect whether it floats or sinks; use force diagrams to describe the effects of forces; make predictions and check them; plan a fair test and identify the independent, dependent and control variables; take accurate measurements; record observations in tables; describe patterns in results; draw conclusions; suggest changes that improve a boat design; present results in a scatter graph. Key words: displaced, upthrust, gravity, float, sink, mass, shape, variable, scatter graph.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — testing a cork and a coin, comparing the force arrows on a floating and a sinking ball, adding 10 g spoonfuls of sand to a container until it sinks at 50 g (the book's own test), naming the variables of that fair test, testing how shape changes floating with a marble and foil boats, then sorting Marcus and Arun's real results (base areas 2–16 cm², 1–9 coins) to find the pattern. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: an object floats when its mass is less than or equal to the mass of water it displaces, and a wider shape displaces more water, so it can carry more mass.
