    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 5 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742955), Unit 5 Forces and magnetism, section 5.6 Magnetic force, pages 128–135; Workbook 5 (ISBN 9781108742962), pages 79–83. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: see that forces act over a distance between magnets, and between a magnet and a magnetic material; know that magnets can have different magnetic strengths; plan a fair test and identify the independent, dependent and control variables; take accurate measurements, repeat them for reliability, record results in a table, present them on a bar graph and make a conclusion. Key words: magnetic force, magnet, magnetic material, attract, repel, pole, strength, variable.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — sorting the book's own ideas, tapping the scene, or running the pole test on the bench. The strength test reproduces the Workbook's own example results exactly (Magnet 1: N 35/28/30 mm, mean 31; S 31/26/27 mm, mean 28 — Magnet 2: N 51/46/53 mm, mean 50; S 54/49/53 mm, mean 52), and the console table fills in only when the learner runs that pole's test. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. The open research question in the Workbook Challenge (which countries use Maglev trains) is not in the exercises, because it has no single correct answer; the class project on page 133 of the Learner's Book is a visit-and-report task and is likewise left for the classroom.
