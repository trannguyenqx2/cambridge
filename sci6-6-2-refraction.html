    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 6 Light and the Solar System, section 6.2, pages 149–154; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 82–85. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: describe how a ray of light changes direction when it travels through different mediums and know that this is called refraction; use diagrams to show how light refracts; ask scientific questions and select appropriate enquiries; make predictions and check them; use secondary sources to find answers; make conclusions from results using knowledge of refraction. Key words: converge, convex lens, focal point, lens, prism, rainbow, refraction, refracts, medium, optical illusion.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — setting up the arrows-behind-the-glass test, exploring the pencil and fish optical illusions and judging the pencil question, sorting light journeys into refraction and not refraction, tapping the three lens things, tapping the four sky things, then sorting lens jobs from prism and rainbow jobs. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: light refracts only when it moves between different mediums, the pencil and the desert mirage are optical illusions (the things are not really where or how they look), a convex lens bends rays inwards to a focal point, and a prism or raindrop splits white light because each colour refracts at a different angle.
