    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 1 The human body, section 1.1, pages 2–11; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 2–5. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The section's closing research and class-presentation tasks (other factors that affect pulse rate; asking and investigating your own question) are classroom research tasks and are not part of this interactive lesson.

    Objectives: describe the parts of the circulatory system and their functions; say how the circulatory systems of other animals are similar to ours; measure pulse rates and record results in tables; make a prediction and plan a fair test on the effect of exercise on pulse rate; use results to say if the prediction was accurate, describe patterns and make a conclusion. Key words: circulation, circulatory system, heart, blood, blood vessels, carbon dioxide, oxygen, pressure, pulse.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the body models on the 3D stage itself, sorting cards, or choosing a prediction. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the stage' panel only move the camera; meeting a model means tapping it on the stage. The examples follow the book's order: the three main parts first, then the two sides of the heart, the three kinds of blood vessel, the circulatory systems of a fish, a frog and a bird, feeling a pulse at the wrist and neck, and finally a fair-test prediction about exercise and pulse rate, using the Workbook's Marcus results (sitting 72, digging 80, cycling 110, running 120, football 150 beats per minute).
