    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 9 Electricity, section 9.2 Current and voltage in parallel circuits, pages 296–305; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 174–178. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The hands-on Activity 9.1.1 (measuring voltage in a series circuit with a real voltmeter) and the full Think like a scientist investigation (measuring current in a parallel circuit, repeating readings and plotting a graph) are practical bench work and are represented here by the bench models and the book’s own example values, not reproduced as wet practicals.

    Objectives (from the book): compare current and voltage in series and parallel circuits; describe the effects of adding cells and lamps on current and voltage in circuits. Key words: rating, voltage, voltmeter, volts — plus current, ammeter, series, parallel, supply and branch.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D circuit bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the circuit bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: what voltage is (1.5 V cells, series cells adding up, ratings and mains 220–240 V), how it is measured (voltmeter in parallel, ammeter in series), series voltages adding to the supply (the book's 6 V lamp + 3 V buzzer on a 9 V battery; three identical lamps sharing 12 V as 4 V each), parallel branch voltages equalling the supply (9 V across lamp and buzzer alike), and the effects of adding branches or adding components inside a branch (the book's 1 A → 2 A parallel example). The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks: 9.2A meters and the 1.5 V / 6 V parallel questions, 9.2B-style calculations including Zara's 7 V buzzer, Arun's 2 V lamp, the 220 V classroom lamps at 0.4 A each and Marcus's six 2.0 V lamps in series on 12 V, and 9.2C series/parallel effects including VL = VC ÷ N and VL = VC.
