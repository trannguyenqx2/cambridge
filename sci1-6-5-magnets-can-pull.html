    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 1 (2nd edition), Unit 6 Forces, section 6.5, pages 133–136; Workbook 1, pages 77–79. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: investigate how magnets pull on magnetic materials; make predictions about which materials are magnetic; record observations in a table. Key words: magnet, magnetic, non-magnetic, attract/attracted, pull, metal, predict.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — most of them by tapping things on the 3D science table itself. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the table' panel only move the camera to look at a thing; testing means tapping the thing on the table. The big idea of this section: a magnet pulls magnetic materials (metal objects such as keys, nails and coins), and we say they are attracted; wood, plastic, paper and rubber are non-magnetic, so the magnet cannot pull them — and not even a paper clip tied on a string escapes the pull!
