    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 8 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742825), Unit 4 Ecosystems, section 4.3, pages 134–136; Workbook 8 (ISBN 9781108742856), pages 76–81. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: explain how new or invasive species can affect an ecosystem, using the New Zealand stoats and rats, Sonoran buffelgrass, South American beavers, water hyacinth and Australian cane toads as examples. Key words: native species, invasive species, introduced species, extinct, eradicate.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action on the 3D stage — meeting the saguaro and its fast newcomer, sorting New Zealand animals into native and introduced, meeting the rat and the stoat, finding why the flightless kiwi and its ground nest are so vulnerable, investigating buffelgrass, and weighing eradication against control. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done.
