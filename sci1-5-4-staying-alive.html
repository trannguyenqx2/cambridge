    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 1 (2nd edition), Unit 5 Humans, section 5.4, pages 99–104; Workbook 1, pages 61–63. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: find out what humans and other animals need to stay alive; learn which foods are healthy; make a prediction, test it by tasting, and say if the prediction was right or wrong. Key words: air, breathe, gills, healthy, unhealthy.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action. In the scene, the learner taps the food, water and air a child needs, watches a diver breathe from an air bottle and a shark breathe with its gills, sorts healthy and unhealthy foods, packs a bag for a trip to the stars, predicts which healthy foods they will like and then 'tastes' them, and finally builds a healthy salad. The dark 'Show me' line checks the action itself, and answers stay locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Remember the science: toys are fun, but they do not keep anyone alive; and a wrong prediction is not a failure — it is how we learn something new.
