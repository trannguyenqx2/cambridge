    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 2 Materials: properties and changes, section 2.2, pages 42–48; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 23–27. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: learn that conducting heat and electricity are properties of a substance; make predictions and check them against results; record observations in tables; work safely in practical investigations; suggest how to improve an investigation; draw conclusions and spot patterns in measurements; group substances by their properties and say which type of scientific enquiry an investigation uses. Key words: conduction, thermal conductor, electrical conductor, insulator, property.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action on the 3D bench. The bead race uses the book's own investigation materials (metal spoon, glass rod, plastic spoon, pencil, polystyrene foam, beads on petroleum jelly in hot water); the circuit test uses the book's substances and its tick scale (no ticks = lamp off, one tick = dim, two ticks = bright). The pattern step reproduces the book's Activity 1 measurements exactly (aluminium 237/37, copper 386/59, graphite 168/1, brass 150/16, steel 80/10). The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line) and the conclusion stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Safety note from the book: in real life, never touch hot water and take care not to knock the beaker over.
