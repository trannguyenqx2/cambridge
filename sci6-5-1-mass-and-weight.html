    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 5 Forces and electricity, section 5.1, pages 112–118; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 60–61. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: describe the difference between mass measured in kilograms (kg) and weight measured in newtons (N); describe the effect of gravity and know that when gravity changes, the weight of an object changes but the mass does not; measure the mass and weight of objects and practise pattern seeking; decide when it is necessary to repeat measurements to give more reliable data; describe a pattern in results and identify results that do not fit the pattern; make a conclusion from results using science knowledge; record measurements in a table. Key words: astronaut, forcemeter, mass, newton, rehabilitation, weight, weightlessness.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — finding the scale and the forcemeter, sorting ideas into mass and weight, visiting one astronaut on Earth, the Moon and Jupiter, measuring three objects and recording them in a table, spotting the one result that breaks the pattern and repeating it, then sorting the effects of weightlessness from the ways astronauts cope with it. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: on Earth, 1 kilogram of mass has a weight of about 10 newtons, and a pull added to a forcemeter makes its reading wrong, because the reading must show gravity's pull alone.
