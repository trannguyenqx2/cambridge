    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 5 Forces and electricity, section 5.4, pages 130–141; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 74–77. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: use conventional symbols to draw circuit diagrams; choose components for circuits with batteries, switches and buzzers; compare the brightness of lamps in series and parallel circuits; make predictions about electricity and test them; describe risks in practical work and how to reduce them; repeat observations when unsure; present results in tables; draw conclusions from results; identify and classify circuits as series or parallel. Key words: circuit diagram, conventional symbol, parallel circuit, series circuit, symbol, volt, voltage.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — naming the components of the book's starting circuit, tapping the symbol plaques for a cell, a lamp, a buzzer and an open switch, choosing the right energy source for a lamp circuit and for a 3 V buzzer circuit, testing a series circuit by lighting both lamps and then unscrewing one, testing a parallel circuit the same way, and finally sorting circuit descriptions into series and parallel. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: a series circuit has only one path, so removing one lamp breaks the whole circuit; in a parallel circuit each lamp has its own path and receives the full voltage, so the lamps shine brighter and one lamp can be removed without affecting the other.
