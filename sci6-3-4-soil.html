    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 3 Rocks, the rock cycle and soil, section 3.4, pages 87–96; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 46–50. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: find out that there are different types of soils and that they can be classified based on their clay, sand and organic content; find out that the composition of soil can change, making it better or worse for plants to grow in it; make a prediction and see whether observation supports the prediction; observe and sort different materials in soil; record observations in a table. Key words: artificial fertiliser, composition, compost, loam, organic matter, nutrient, pesticide, soil, texture, waterlogged.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — investigating a soil sample spread on newspaper, sorting the materials found in soil, identifying the three soil types from their descriptions, sorting soil clues, tapping the things that improve soil, then sorting actions that help or damage the soil. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: loam has the best composition for growing plants, compost improves texture as well as adding nutrients, and pesticides and planting the same crop every year damage the soil.
