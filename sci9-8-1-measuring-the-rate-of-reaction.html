    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 8 Rates of reaction, section 8.1 Measuring the rate of reaction, pages 260–267; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 147–153. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. Activity 8.1.1 (making a poster about what makes a good graph) and the full Think like a scientist practical — writing your own equipment list, risk assessment and results table — are classroom tasks and are not part of this interactive lesson; the investigation itself is represented here by the reaction bench in steps 3 and 5.

    Objectives (from the book): learn how the rate of reaction can change; measure the rate of reaction; use graphs to discuss and measure the rate of reaction; explain why the rate of reaction changes. Key words: anomalous result, collecting a gas over water, collisions, gradient, rate of reaction.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D reaction bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the reaction bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: magnesium and dilute sulfuric acid giving off hydrogen into a gas syringe; the book's own results table, which levels off at 66 cm³ from 270 s, with the anomalous 26 cm³ at 90 s (about 32 cm³ expected from the curve); mass loss as carbon dioxide escapes from calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid; the gradient as the measure of rate, with the book's copper carbonate calculation (20 cm³ in 20 s between 10 s and 30 s = 1 cm³ per second); collecting a gas over water with readings every 30 seconds until three readings are the same; and collisions explaining why the rate falls. The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks and numbers: 8.1A reading the slope of the magnesium and hydrochloric acid graph, 8.1B Sofia's three repeated runs and the word equations, and 8.1C Marcus's measuring-cylinder results — including his anomalous 10 cm³ at 150 s — judged for reliability and sources of error.
