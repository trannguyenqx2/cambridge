    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 8 Rates of reaction, section 8.3 Temperature and the rate of reaction, pages 273–277; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 158–162. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives (from the book): investigate the effect of temperature on the rate of reaction; do a trial run and preliminary work for the investigation and use the information to improve the investigation method; select equipment; plot a graph of results. Key words in the book's list are drawn from the unit; this lesson's glossary covers rate of reaction, precipitate, trial run, preliminary work, water bath and collision.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D reaction bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the reaction bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the sodium thiosulfate and hydrochloric acid trial run (10 cm³ of thiosulfate solution, 1 cm³ of acid, time until the cross can no longer be seen, with the book's safety points), the preliminary work (choosing a range and temperature steps, warming in a water bath), the investigation itself, the book's typical-results graph for marble chips with acid at 30 °C and 40 °C (same final volume of carbon dioxide, reached sooner at the higher temperature), and the particle-theory explanation. The bench times at 20/40/60 °C are illustrative simulation times, not book data — the book gives no numerical table for this reaction. The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks: 8.3A explaining with particle theory why the rate is high at the start and slows down, 8.3B planning Sofia and Marcus's metal-and-acid investigation (20 °C to 70 °C, interval 10 °C, timing until the fizzing stops), and 8.3C predicting the trend of time against temperature.
