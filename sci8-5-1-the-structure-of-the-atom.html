    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 8 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742825), Unit 5 Materials and their structure, section 5.1, pages 147–151; Workbook 8 (ISBN 9781108742856), pages 88–90. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: describe the structure of the atom; list the particles found in an atom; describe some of the properties of the particles found in an atom; describe some of the discoveries that led to the model of the atom used today. Key words: atoms, sub-atomic particles, protons, neutrons, electrons, nucleus, electrical charge, electrostatic attraction, deflected.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action in the 3D atom lab — finding the three sub-atomic particles in a helium atom, sorting them by mass, running Rutherford's gold foil experiment (most particles pass straight through; about 1 in 8000 is deflected), tracing the Thomson, Rutherford and Chadwick discoveries, counting helium's charges (+1 +1 −1 −1 = 0), and sorting true facts from false ones. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. The Workbook's drawing tasks (labelling a diagram, drawing the Thomson and Rutherford models, completing the gold foil diagram) are turned into tap-and-sort tasks with the same content.
