    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 9 Electricity, section 9.3 Resistance, pages 306-311; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 179-183. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. Activity 9.3.1 (measuring the voltage across and current through real components such as a lamp, a buzzer and a resistor) is represented here by the component models on the bench in step 4 and the calculations in the exercises.

    Objectives (from the book): discover how resistance affects current in a circuit; calculate resistance from voltage and current. Key words: resistance, ohms, Ohm’s law, resistor, filament.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action - tapping the models on the 3D electricity bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark Show me line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. Show me how only demonstrates - it never counts as done. Buttons in the On the electricity bench panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book order: what resistance means, then the rule that greater resistance means smaller current, then Ohm’s law V = I x R and the book worked example (a buzzer at 10 V and 2 A has a resistance of 5 ohms), then measuring real components as in Activity 9.3.1, and finally the book investigation with a resistor of about 10 ohms. The exercises use the Workbook tasks and numbers from 9.3A, 9.3B and 9.3C.
