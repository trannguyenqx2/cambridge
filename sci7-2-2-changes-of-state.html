    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 7 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742788), Unit 2 Materials and their structure, section 2.2 Changes of state, pages 29–34; Workbook 7 (ISBN 9781108742818), section 2.2, pages 21–25. The particle-model opener follows the section's Getting started task. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: draw and describe the particle structure of a solid, a liquid and a gas; name the changes of state (melt, freeze, condense, boil); measure the volume and the temperature of a liquid accurately with a measuring cylinder and a thermometer; investigate how the temperature of water changes as it is heated, and record the results in a table. Key words: boil, boiling point, change of state, condensation, condense, evaporation, freeze, measuring cylinder, melt, melting point, meniscus, steam, thermometer, water vapour.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action on the 3D lab bench or with the sorting cards. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. In the heating investigation the thermometer readings are fixed (20, 45, 70, then 100 °C) so the record table always matches the screen; the Workbook exercises reuse Marcus's table (20 °C rising to 88 °C over 5 minutes) and Zara's graph, including her one point that does not fit the pattern. Watch out for the two classic traps: reading the top instead of the bottom of the meniscus, and expecting the temperature to keep rising while the water boils — it stays at 100 °C.
