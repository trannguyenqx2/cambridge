    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 5 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742955), Unit 5 Forces and magnetism, section 5.2 Gravity and satellites, pages 108–111; Workbook 5 (ISBN 9781108742962), pages 64–67. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: identify the force gravity; use a model to see how gravity keeps a satellite in orbit; know that a satellite is an object in space that orbits a larger object; use a force diagram to show the name and direction of forces acting on an object. Key words: gravity, satellite, natural satellite, artificial satellite, orbit, force, rocket.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action in the space model — tapping the satellites, pointing to parts of the book's Earth–Moon force diagram, choosing the bigger pull arrow, finding the path the Moon would take with no gravity, sorting natural and artificial satellites, and launching a satellite at the Workbook's orbit speed of 27,200 km/h (slower: it falls back; faster than 37,600 km/h: it leaves Earth's gravity). The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Weather satellites photographing cyclones, TV and GPS satellites and the International Space Station are the book's examples of artificial satellites at work.
