    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 1 (2nd edition), Unit 3 Materials in my world, section 3.4, pages 53–57; Workbook 1, pages 34–36. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: find out which materials can be stretched, squashed (compressed), twisted or bent; measure how far rubber bands stretch with bricks; make a prediction and say if it was right. Key words: stretch, squash, compress, twist, bend, elastic, rigid, measure, shapes.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action on the test bench. The results grid fills in only when the child really tries an action on a material, and each answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Three ideas to watch: squash and compress are two names for the same action; an elastic material goes back to its old shape after stretching (the bent paper does not); and a rigid material like wood does not change shape at all, so its whole row is ✗. In the band investigation every band carries the same 3 bricks — the thin band stretches most (5 brick-units), then the middle band (3), then the thick band (2).
