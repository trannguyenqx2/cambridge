    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 7 Genes and inheritance, section 7.1 Chromosomes, genes and DNA, pages 234–237; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 131–132. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. Activity 7.1.1 (making model chromosomes for a chosen species out of string or wool on a paper cell) is a hands-on classroom modelling task; it is represented here by the species chromosome-count models on the 3D bench in step 3. The Workbook Focus exercise asks learners to name two labelled parts of the plant-cell and animal-cell diagrams and to mark where the chromosomes are; because the label positions live only in the printed diagrams, that exercise is represented here by the 'inside the nucleus or not' sorting task in Exercise 1.

    Objectives (from the book): learn that chromosomes contain genes; find out what genes and chromosomes are made of. Key words: chromosomes, DNA, genes.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D cell bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the cell bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the Getting started puzzle of the two nuclei (an atom's nucleus and a cell's nucleus), chromosomes as threads inside the cell nucleus that show up — stained — only when a cell divides, the book's own chromosome counts (humans 46, fruit flies 8, mango trees 40) with chromosomes numbered by length and held in pairs, genes arranged in sequence along each chromosome (about 2000 genes on chromosome 1, about 600 on chromosome 15, with two eye-colour genes on chromosome 15), and finally DNA: each chromosome is one enormously long DNA molecule shaped like a twisted ladder — a double helix — and one gene is a length of about 2500 twists. The closing sort checks the book's summary: chromosomes are made of DNA, and each chromosome contains many different genes. The exercises follow the Workbook: Focus on where chromosomes are found (inside the nucleus — and why a red blood cell has none), Practice with sentences and the book's numbers for gene, DNA and chromosome, and the Challenge of why all the DNA is copied before a cell divides.
