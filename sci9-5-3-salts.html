    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 5 Reactivity, section 5.3 Salts, pages 171–177; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 92–94. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. Activity 5.3.1 (researching a salt and presenting the findings as a poster, talk or slide show) is a classroom research task and is not part of this interactive lesson.

    Objectives (from the book): explain what a salt is; give some examples of salts and their uses; prepare a salt by reacting a metal with an acid; prepare a salt by heating a metal oxide with an acid. Key words: carbonate, chloride, citrates, crystallisation, formula (plural: formulae), nitrate, salt, sulfate.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the jars, bottles and apparatus on the 3D lab bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; doing a step means tapping the object on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: salts and their uses first, then the acid → salt families from the book's own table (hydrochloric acid HCl → chlorides, sulfuric acid H2SO4 → sulfates, nitric acid HNO3 → nitrates), then the book's zinc sulfate practical with its own quantities (about 50 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid, 1–5 g of zinc), gentle evaporation and crystallisation, and finally copper sulfate from copper oxide, with filtration. The Getting started displacement-equation swap is a partner writing task from the previous sections and is recalled inside step 6's method choices rather than replayed. The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks: 5.3A linking acids, formulae and salt families, 5.3B word equations and the silver / potassium method questions, and 5.3C the three practical steps for copper chloride crystals.
