    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 3 Forces and energy, section 3.5 Ways of transferring thermal energy, pages 111–121; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 58–61. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives (from the book): describe how thermal energy can be transferred by conduction; describe how thermal energy can be transferred by convection; describe how thermal energy can be transferred by radiation. Key words: conduction, convection, convection current, expand, radiation, vigorously — plus emit.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; testing a transfer means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: conduction through a solid (the rod investigation with wax and paper clips, and the metal pan), conductors and insulators (plastic handle, wooden spoon, woollen clothes), convection in water (Activity 3.5.1's coloured ink over a flame) and in air (the room heater), radiation from the Sun across a vacuum, the emitting investigation with dull black, white and silver cans, and finally all three ways together — the room heater and the vacuum flask, whose vacuum stops conduction and whose silvered wall reflects radiation.
