    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 5 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742955), Unit 5 Forces and magnetism, section 5.4, pages 118–122; Workbook 5 (ISBN 9781108742962), pages 72–74. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: identify gravity, applied forces, normal forces, upthrust, friction, air resistance and water resistance; see that an object may have multiple forces acting on it at rest and when moving; make and test a paper aeroplane; use force diagrams; make predictions and say how accurate they were; suggest how a paper aeroplane could be improved. Key words: multiple, thrust.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping things in the 3D forces park or sorting force cards. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'In the forces park' panel only move the camera; doing a step means tapping the thing itself in the park. Note: the paper aeroplane here is a simulation of the book's make-and-test activity — folding a real sheet of A4 paper and throwing it in class makes a good follow-up. The tricky ideas: a resting object still has forces on it (gravity and the normal force balance), and thrust is an applied force.
