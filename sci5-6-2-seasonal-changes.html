    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 5 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742955), Unit 6 Seasons and adaptations, section 6.2, pages 142–148; Workbook 5 (ISBN 9781108742962), pages 88–92. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The Cape Town sunrise and sunset values on the data panel are the book's own example values.

    Objectives: describe how the tilt of the Earth can create different seasons in different places; make predictions about length of day; describe patterns in data; present and interpret data; use different sources to find information; make a conclusion based on scientific understanding. Key words: sunrise, sunset, season, adapt, chlorophyll, dormant, hibernate, hibernation, migrate, migration.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the Sun's path, sorting the data pattern, tapping the tree through autumn, meeting the hibernating bear and the migrating bird and whale, and a final hibernate-or-migrate sort. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Two ideas to watch: the Sun only SEEMS to move across the sky (the Earth spins), and Cape Town is in the southern half of the Earth, so its February days are long summer days that get shorter towards autumn. The exercises use the Workbook's own Karachi table, Doha graph questions and Arctic tern story, in submit mode.
