    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 7 Genes and inheritance, section 7.2 Gametes and inheritance, pages 238–243; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 133–134. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. Activity 7.2.1 (modelling sex inheritance with coloured strings — 30 X strings in the egg pot, 15 X and 15 Y in the sperm pot) is represented here by the string-pot models and tally board in step 6.

    Objectives (from the book): learn what a gamete is; think about the similarities and differences between male and female gametes; find out what happens at fertilisation; explain what determines whether a baby is a boy or a girl. Key words: egg cell, fertilisation, gametes, inheritance, sex inheritance, sperm cell, zygote.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D genetics bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the genetics bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: meeting the two gametes, comparing sperm and egg cells (the book's comparison-table question), fertilisation and the zygote with 46 chromosomes, the X and Y sex chromosomes, why the sperm decides boy or girl and why the numbers born are about equal, and finally Activity 7.2.1 — modelling sex inheritance with the book's own string counts (30 X in the egg pot; 15 X and 15 Y in the sperm pot). The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks for 7.2: the comparison of the two gametes from the labelled diagrams, the sentence completions using its word list (cytoplasm, female, fertilisation, gametes, male, nucleus, swimming), and the Challenge of explaining how the differences help each gamete do its job.
