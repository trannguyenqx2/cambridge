    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 4 Maintaining life, section 4.1 Plants and water, pages 134–139; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 66–71. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The wall-display self-assessment after Activity 4.1.1 is a classroom peer-comparison task and is not part of this interactive lesson.

    Objectives (from the book): find out how plants absorb water; discover how water moves from a plant's roots to its leaves. Key words: root hairs, xylem vessels — plus mineral ions, nitrate ions, magnesium ions and cell sap.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; to test something, tap the model on the bench itself. The steps follow the book's order: water between soil particles enters a root hair (with dissolved nitrate and magnesium ions), crosses the root to the xylem in its centre, then the celery-stalk dye investigation from the book — stalk in 1–2 cm of coloured water, clean cut end, rinse before cutting across, and the blue spots in the cut face are xylem. The exercises use the Workbook's own data: orange seedlings (variety A: 920 root hairs, 0.03 mm; variety B: 800, 0.02 mm), Zara's temperature results (the 50 °C value, 3.2 cm, is the anomalous one) and the wheat varieties at 2 °C.
