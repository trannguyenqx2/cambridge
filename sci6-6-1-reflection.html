    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 6 Light and the Solar System, section 6.1, pages 142–148; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 78–81. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: describe how a ray of light changes direction when it is reflected from a plane mirror; use diagrams to show how light reflects from a plane mirror; decide when observations need to be repeated to give more reliable data; describe a pattern in results; make a conclusion from results using knowledge of reflection; suggest improvements to the design of a periscope. Key words: angle, incident ray, mirror image, normal, periscope, plane mirror, reflected ray.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the three surfaces that reflect light well, tracing light from the Sun to the tree to the eye in order, sorting ray-diagram labels, repeating the torch-and-mirror test at three angles just as in the book's investigation, tracing light through a periscope in order, then sorting correct from incorrect reflection diagrams. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: angles are measured from the normal, the reflected angle always equals the incident angle, and periscope mirrors sit at 45 degrees.
