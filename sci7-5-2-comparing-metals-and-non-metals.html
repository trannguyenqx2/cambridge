    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 7 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742788), Unit 5 Properties of materials, section 5.2, pages 151–154; Workbook 7 (ISBN 9781108742818), pages 93–97. All wording is rewritten in our own words.

    Objectives: compare the properties of metals and non-metals; investigate materials and decide if they are metals or non-metals. Key words: contact, distinguish, examine, ductile, sonorous, malleable, brittle.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — sorting the book's comparison properties into metals and non-metals, defining the four Getting started words (ductile, sonorous, malleable, brittle), meeting mercury, the metal that is liquid at room temperature, checking a test circuit the book's way (touch the crocodile clips together first, so you know the lamp works, and make good contact with the test material), testing iron, copper, carbon and sulfur on the bench, and finally deciding metal or non-metal for six materials. Watch out for carbon (graphite): it is a non-metal that conducts electricity, so it can be hard to place. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done.
