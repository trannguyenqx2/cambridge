    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 7 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742788), Unit 4 Grouping and identifying organisms, section 4.2, pages 124–127; Workbook 7 (ISBN 9781108742818), pages 75–76. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: learn about the structure of a virus; discuss whether viruses are non-living or living. Key words: electron microscope, influenza, protein, replicate, RNA, virus.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action on the 3D bench — comparing a school microscope, an electron microscope and a living cell to grasp how small a virus is (the book photograph magnifies viruses 100 000 times); opening a virus to find RNA threads inside its protein coat; collecting the book's model materials (modelling clay = protein coat, torn paper = RNA threads, push pins = pegs); following replication inside a living cell; meeting H3N2 influenza, SARS-CoV-2 (Covid-19, 2019) and plant viruses; and finally testing a virus against the seven characteristics of living things (the Getting started list). The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. The Workbook Challenge's open internet research is adapted in the exercises as guided source-choice questions (1892 discovery), because the lesson itself has no web access.
