    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 8 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742825), Unit 2 Properties of materials, section 2.2, pages 39–46; Workbook 8 (ISBN 9781108742856), pages 23–27. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives (from the book): make solutions of different concentrations; compare the number of solute particles in solutions of different concentrations; investigate solubility; compare the solubility of various solutes. Key words: concentrated, dilute, insoluble, saturated, solubility, soluble, solution.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — making a solution from a solute and a solvent, sorting soluble and insoluble solids, picking the most and least concentrated food-dye solutions (the book table: A = 10 cm3 dye + 0 cm3 water, B = 8 + 2, C = 6 + 4, D = 4 + 6, E = 2 + 8, each totalling 10 cm3), adding spatulas of salt until the solution is saturated, comparing how much sugar dissolves in 100 g of water at 20 °C (204 g) and at 80 °C (362 g), and using the book worked example to calculate the sugar for 250 g of water (510 g at 20 °C). The lesson checks the action itself (the dark Show me line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. Show me how only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Book solubility table, grams per 100 g of water at 20 °C: sodium chloride 36, copper sulfate 32, calcium chloride 74, potassium chlorate 7, lead chloride 1.
