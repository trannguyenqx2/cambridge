    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 7 Genes and inheritance, section 7.3 Variation, pages 244–250; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 135–140. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The three-generation family photograph, the wrist-circumference class investigation (shown here through its grouping idea and the frequency-diagram rule in step 6 and the exercises) and the section summary checklist discussion are classroom material; the checklist itself closes step 6. The holly-leaf photograph in Workbook 7.3B cannot be counted from the text pack, so the Practice calculations use the Learner's Book leaflet data instead.

    Objectives (from the book): learn what is meant by variation; think about how genes help to produce variation; investigate and record some examples of variation. Key words: variation, genetic differences — plus species, genes, DNA, fertilisation, environment, tally, median, mode and mean.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D garden bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the garden bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: variation in goats of one species (with the Stage 7 definition of a species in the Getting started opener), measuring variation in humans (Activity 7.3.1 — hair colour, eye colour, shoe size, height in cm for at least eight people in the book; three people modelled here), Zara's kidney vetch tally and bar chart (red 5, pink 2, orange 3, yellow 9, cream 3 — 22 plants, yellow most common), DNA and genetic differences at fertilisation, genes-only versus genes-plus-environment variation (goat coat colour versus body mass), and the leaflet investigation with median, mode and mean, closing with the book's summary checklist. The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks: 7.3A the canna lily tally (yellow 5, white 3, red 4, orange 6 — 18 plants, with flower colour on the horizontal axis of the bar chart), 7.3B calculations on the book's kidney vetch results and a leaflet data set that begins with the book's own example values 11, 15, 12, 11, 13, plus the wrist frequency-diagram rule (equal ranges, bars touching), and 7.3C choosing a pea-pod feature that can be counted or measured.
