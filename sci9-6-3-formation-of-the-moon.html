    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 6 Sound and space, section 6.3 Formation of the Moon, pages 216–220; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 117–120. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. Excluded from this interactive lesson: Activity 6.3.1 (a group internet-research report on the collision theory, including judging website reliability and bias) — a classroom research task, not an interactive activity; and the sound-wave items at the top of the Workbook pack page, which are the tail of section 6.2, not part of 6.3.

    Objectives (from the book): describe evidence for the collision theory for the formation of the Moon. Key words: collision theory / giant impact hypothesis, Theia, gravity — plus fission theory, evidence, molten, dense and iron core.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D planetarium table itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'In the planetarium' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the table. The steps follow the book's order: the Getting started discussion of how the Solar System formed from a cloud of dust and gas; the older fission idea and the measured drift of the Moon (about 4 cm a year); the 1974 collision theory — Theia, about the size of Mars, striking the young Earth relatively soon after the Solar System formed; the ring of rocks and dust pulled together by gravity; and finally the book's own two-column table of evidence that supports the theory and evidence that contradicts it, which the learner sorts in the last step. The exercises use the Workbook's own tasks: 6.3A (stage order, what Theia was, which bodies hold Theia material — recast here as theory sorting), 6.3B (the 4600-million-year timeline and describing the theory, with the book's questions 1–4), and 6.3C (three supporting pieces of evidence, one contradicting piece, and the evidence that fits both the fission and collision theories).
