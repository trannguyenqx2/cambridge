    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 8 (2nd edition; ISBN 9781108742825), Unit 2 Properties of materials, section 2.3, pages 47–50; Workbook 8 (ISBN 9781108742856), pages 28–32. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: plan an investigation, considering all the variables; carry out an investigation. Key words: variables, independent variable, dependent variable, control variables, range, interval.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — spotting the three parts of the students' plan on the bench, sorting the variables into independent, dependent and control, choosing a practical range (20 °C to 80 °C) and interval (every 10 °C), carrying out the test at the two ends of the range in 50 cm³ of water, plotting the results, and labelling the axes the way the book prescribes (independent variable along the horizontal axis, dependent variable up the vertical axis). The book's plan counts spatulas of salt (sodium chloride) dissolving in 50 cm³ of water at temperatures from 20 °C to 80 °C; the simulation's own counts at the two ends of the range are 4 spatulas at 20 °C and 9 spatulas at 80 °C, stated to the learner in the step itself. The Workbook exercises use the Workbook's own results table (mass of salt dissolving in volumes of water from 10 cm³ to 100 cm³). The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Safety note from the book's planning questions: the plan must say how you will work safely — in this simulation, never touch hot water.
