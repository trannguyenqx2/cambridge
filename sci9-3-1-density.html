    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 3 Forces and energy, section 3.1 Density, pages 77–90; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 42–45. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The book's end-of-section questions on seawater, copper rearrangements, liquid layers and party balloons, its oil-spill pollution context and the neutronium fun fact are reading and discussion material and are not separate interactive steps here; the liquid-layer and balloon ideas are covered in the step conclusions.

    Objectives (from the book): discover what is meant by density; find out how to calculate density; compare the densities of solids, liquids and gases; understand and predict whether objects will float or sink in water. Key words: density, hollow, irregular, regular, solid — plus volume, mass and displacement.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; measuring a material means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order and its own example values: the regular wood block 10 × 6 × 4 mm = 240 mm³, the irregular rock raising the cylinder water from 40 cm³ to 56 cm³ (volume 16 cm³), the 1 cm³ cubes of iron (7.9 g) and polystyrene (0.05 g), steel ships (steel 8–9 g/cm³) floating by average density, and the book's float-or-sink densities (pine wood 0.41, polyethene 0.95, cooking oil 0.91, polycarbonate 1.2, aluminium 2.7, iron 7.9 g/cm³ against water 1.0 g/cm³). Activities 3.1.1 and the displacement investigation are represented by steps 2–4 on the bench.
