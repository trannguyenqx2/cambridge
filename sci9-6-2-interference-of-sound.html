    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 6 Sound and space, section 6.2 Interference of sound, pages 207–215; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 112–116. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The Getting started paper task (drawing waves and sorting the drawings by amplitude and then by frequency) is a group paper activity and is not part of this interactive lesson; the class water-tray activity 6.2.1 is represented by step 1's two wave makers in one tray.

    Objectives (from the book): find out how sound waves can reinforce each other to make louder sounds; find out how sound waves can cancel each other out to make no sound. Key words: interference, reinforce — plus cancel, amplitude, frequency, peak, trough and out of phase.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D sound bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the sound bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: water waves as an analogy for interference, waves that reinforce (peaks and troughs together — amplitudes add, frequency stays the same, the sound is louder), waves that cancel (a peak meets an equal trough and the amplitudes add to zero), noise-cancelling headphones, and the tuning-fork and pipe investigation, where the reflected wave reinforces the source wave and a higher frequency needs a shorter pipe. The book's own numbers are used: two 450 Hz waves of 0.5 mm amplitude reinforce to 450 Hz and 1.0 mm, and a 600 Hz wave of amplitude 0.25 mm needs an equal 0.25 mm wave to cancel to zero.
