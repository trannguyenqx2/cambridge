    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 9 Electricity, section 9.4 Practical circuits, pages 312–318; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 184–187. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The section project (building a model school or house with circuits for its rooms) and the unit check-your-progress pages are classroom making and review tasks and are not part of this interactive lesson. The book's paper activity — drawing circuit diagrams for Circuits 1–4 and then building them with real components — is represented here by the design sort in step 6 and the exercises.

    Objectives (from the book): draw circuit diagrams that include symbols for cells, switches, resistors, variable resistors, ammeters, voltmeters, lamps and buzzers; and make circuits that include some or all of these components. Key words: dimmer, fixed resistor, variable resistor, volume.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D circuit bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the circuit bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the Getting started parts and meters (ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel), then fixed and variable resistors (the variable resistor symbol carries an arrow; its resistance can be given as a range such as 1–50 Ω, as in the Workbook), the dimmer circuit (resistance up — current down — lamp dimmer), the book's everyday circuits (the fridge door switch, the car door buzzer that needs the headlamps on as well, the switched voltmeter branch that checks a 12 V battery, and the 6 V lamp run from a 12 V battery across one of two equal series resistors), and finally designing circuits by choosing series loops or parallel branches. The exercises use the Workbook's own structure: 9.4A Focus (the effects of increasing a variable resistor's resistance), 9.4B Practice (using one variable resistor with one lamp, with two lamps in series, and with two lamps in parallel, plus dimmers and volume controls) and 9.4C Challenge (comparing series and parallel circuits, as in the Workbook's circuits A–D).
