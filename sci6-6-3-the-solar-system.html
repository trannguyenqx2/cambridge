    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 6 Light and the Solar System, section 6.3, pages 155–173; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 86 to the end of the book. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: describe the relative position and movement of the planets, the Moon and the Sun in the Solar System; observe and describe the changes in the appearance of the Moon over its monthly cycle; use models, including diagrams, to represent and describe the Solar System; take appropriately accurate measurements; practise the science enquiry skills of research, observation over time and pattern seeking; describe patterns in results, including identifying any results that do not fit the pattern; use a range of secondary information sources to research and answer questions; collect and record observations in a table. Key words: earth day, earth hour, earth year, axis, orbit, phase, waxing, waning.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the Sun and the eight planets in order, choosing how long Earth's two movements take, hunting five record-holding planets, tapping the Moon through five positions of its orbit, then sorting Moon phases into waxing and waning and planet facts among Earth, Mars and Jupiter. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Watch out for the key ideas of this section: sizes and distances in space are far too different to draw on one page (hence the book's 250 m field model), and the Moon's phases come from its orbit around the Earth — the Moon does not really change shape.
