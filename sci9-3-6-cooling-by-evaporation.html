    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 3 Forces and energy, section 3.6 Cooling by evaporation, pages 122–133; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 62–65. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The section's Project (Load lines, pages 129–130) is a group research and modelling project about density and ships, and the Check your progress pages (131–133) review the whole of Unit 3 — neither is part of this section's interactive lesson, so both are excluded here.

    Objective (from the book): understand how evaporation causes cooling. Key words: porous, random — plus evaporation, average energy, thermal energy and humidity.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; testing evaporation means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the Getting started particle discussion (water at about 22 °C; the book's diagram of highest-, medium- and lowest-energy particles), evaporation lowering the average energy and temperature, sweat cooling the skin and the danger when humid air stops it, the porous clay water cooler, the air cooler with a wet sponge and fan (the Think like a scientist practical, including keeping the thermometer dry and unheld), the comparison of perfume, water and liquid soap from Activity 3.6.1 (equal volumes at the same temperature; perfume evaporates fastest and feels coldest, liquid soap slowest), and the book's Summary checklist as a true/false self-check.
