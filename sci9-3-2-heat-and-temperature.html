    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 3 Forces and energy, section 3.2 Heat and temperature, pages 91–98; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 46–49. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The Mpemba freezing investigation in the book needs a real freezer and a long wait, so here it appears as its temperature-difference calculation in the exercises; the measuring investigation (immersion heater, joule meter, thermometer) is simulated on the bench with the book's own procedure.

    Objectives (from the book): understand the difference between heat and temperature. Key words: heat, temperature, thermal energy, joule, particle, temperature difference, absolute zero, joule meter.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the models on the 3D bench itself, or sorting cards into groups. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the bench' panel only move the camera; comparing heat and temperature means tapping the model on the bench. The steps follow the book's order and its own values: the Getting started choice (a 250 cm³ glass of water versus 375 000 litres of pool water, both warmed by 10 °C), the two-glasses comparisons, the ice cream at −20 °C in a 24 °C room (a 44 °C difference), the sparkler at about 1000 °C, absolute zero at −273 °C, and the measuring investigation heated until the temperature rises by 10 °C. Workbook data in the exercises: Sofia's coffee (same volume, 55 °C and 30 °C), Zara's soup (100 cm³ and 200 cm³, both at 60 °C), and Marcus's results table, where the 15 °C reading at 3000 J breaks the pattern (16 °C expected), the heat at 19 °C is about 4500 J and reaching 30 °C needs about 10 000 J.
