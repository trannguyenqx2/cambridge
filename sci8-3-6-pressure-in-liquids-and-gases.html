    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 8 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742825), Unit 3 Forces and energy, section 3.6, pages 100–107; Workbook 8 (ISBN 9781108742856), pages 60–63. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: recall how particles move in liquids and gases; understand how particle movement causes pressure in liquids and gases; predict how changes in liquids and gases affect the pressure. Key words: altitude, atmospheric pressure, collide, container, depth, sea level.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action on the 3D bench — opening the holes of the book's bottle investigation (the jets fly further from the deepest hole), pumping air particles into a balloon, visiting three altitudes, and heating a sealed pressure cooker. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. The final step sorts everyday changes — more air, heating, cooling, squeezing — into pressure rising or falling, which also covers the book's warm-bottle-into-the-fridge activity: cooled air presses less, so the bottle is pushed inwards.
