    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 1 (2nd edition), Unit 2 Sound, section 2.2, pages 26–31; Workbook 1, pages 18–20. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: learn that some sounds are loud and others are quiet; group sounds into loud and quiet; record results in a table; make predictions and check them. Key words: loud, quiet, listen, sense of hearing, damage, instruments, music.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action. Loudness is always shown on the visual sound meter (5 bars) — the lesson never asks the child to judge by ear alone, and any optional sound effect is kept gentle. The dark 'Show me' line checks the action itself, and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Two ideas to watch: covering your ears makes sounds quieter and helps keep ears safe, while a hand by the ear (or big card ears) catches more sound and makes quiet sounds easier to hear.
