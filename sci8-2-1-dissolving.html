    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 8 (2nd edition, ISBN 9781108742825), Unit 2 Properties of materials, section 2.1 Dissolving, pages 34–38; Workbook 8 (ISBN 9781108742856), section 2.1, pages 21–22. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: use the scientific terms for dissolving correctly (solute, solvent, solution, transparent, opaque, conserved); use particle ideas to explain the properties of solutions; practise measuring mass and volume accurately. Key words: conserved, dissolving, opaque, solute, solution, solvent, transparent.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the apparatus on the 3D lab bench, sorting cards, or choosing the correct measuring technique. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the conclusion stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. The mass investigation uses the book's own method: about 20–25 g of salt (22 g here) dissolved in at least 100 cm³ of water, weighed in a beaker on a top pan balance, so the solution's mass equals the solute mass plus the solvent-and-beaker mass. Watch out for the two tricky ideas in this section: a blue solution is still transparent (transparent does not mean colourless), and dissolving is not melting — dissolving always involves two substances.
