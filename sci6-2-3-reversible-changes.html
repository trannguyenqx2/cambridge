    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Primary Science Learner's Book 6 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742979), Unit 2 Materials: properties and changes, section 2.3, pages 49–55; Workbook 6 (ISBN 9781108742986), pages 28–32. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book.

    Objectives: describe changes to substances that are reversible; choose materials and equipment and work safely; use the particle model to explain how temperature affects dissolving; make a prediction, run a fair test, record results in a table and make a conclusion. Key words: irreversible, physical change, solute, solvent, uniform, rate, reversible.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — melting ice in the warm sun and freezing it again, sorting reversible and irreversible changes, adding a solute to a solvent to make a uniform solution, stirring a drink to make the sugar dissolve, timing sugar in cold and hot water in a fair test, and choosing the particle-model sentences that explain the results. The fair test uses the Workbook investigation values: 100 ml of water and one teaspoon of sugar in each jar; at 20 °C the sugar took 90 seconds to dissolve and at 80 °C it took 15 seconds. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Safety note from the book: never touch hot water, and take care not to knock over a jar of hot water.
