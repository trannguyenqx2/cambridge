    For teachers and parents — about this lesson

    Source: Based on Cambridge Lower Secondary Science Learner's Book 9 (2nd edition, 2021; ISBN 9781108742863), Unit 9 Electricity, section 9.1 Parallel circuits, pages 288–295; Workbook 9 (ISBN 9781108742894), pages 171–173. All wording in this lesson is rewritten in our own words; no text is copied from the book. The book's drawn-circuit questions (choosing which drawn circuits are parallel, and drawing series and parallel diagrams for four lamps) are paper tasks and are represented here by the circuit-bench models and the sorting exercises instead.

    Objectives (from the book): find out the difference between series and parallel circuits, and find out how current flows in a parallel circuit. Key words: branches, connected in parallel, connected in series, parallel circuit — plus ammeter, current, cell and switch.

    How it works: each step asks for one real action — tapping the components on the 3D circuit bench itself, or sorting calculation cards. The lesson checks the action itself (the dark 'Show me' line), and the answer stays locked until the action is done. 'Show me how' only demonstrates — it never counts as done. Buttons in the 'On the circuit bench' panel only move the camera; testing an idea means tapping the component on the bench. The steps follow the book's order: the Getting started series circuit and the ammeter, then parallel branches where the current divides equally (2 A + 2 A), the rule that the cell current equals the total of the branch currents (with the book's 1.0 + 2.0 + 0.5 = 3.5 A example), the book's missing-current calculation (X + 3.0 + 1.5 = 5.5, so X = 1.0 A), and the advantages of parallel circuits — independent branch switches S1 and S2 plus a main switch S4, as in car headlamps and strings of lamps. Activity 9.1.1 (measuring at positions A1–A4) is step 3, and the 'Think like a scientist' prediction work is folded into steps 3–4. The exercises use the Workbook's own structure: 9.1A series-or-parallel sorting, 9.1B facts and ammeter readings (including the four-lamp 2.0 A calculation), and 9.1C what changes when the buzzer branch stops working.
